# Tryvo: what to learn from the revised prototype

This is a study plan, not research results. No target-user study has been conducted in this build.

## Primary audience and promise

Recruit five adults aged 18–25 who regularly delegate reasoning tasks to AI, want more opportunities to think independently, and have not established a satisfying practice routine. This is a recruitment hypothesis, not a demographic claim. Ask for recent concrete examples of delegation before showing Tryvo.

The promise to test: an enjoyable investigation in which the player's own reasoning produces an achievement they can identify. Separate enjoyment, personally earned satisfaction, task performance and willingness to return.

## First session: 20–30 minutes

1. Ask about the last task they delegated, what they did themselves, and whether anything felt missing. Do not prime them with claims of cognitive decline or dopamine loss.
2. Let them discover and begin The empty seats. Record where they hesitate, need facilitation, or mistake game mechanics for instructions.
3. Observe the initial judgment, clue choices, interpretations and plan. Do not coach the correct answer. Record whether they understand the scoring and help rules before entering.
4. After commitment, ask them to explain one source–interpretation connection in their own words. Compare that explanation with the structured result; a high score alone is not sufficient evidence of understanding.
5. Let them inspect Your Contribution. Ask: “What changed because of a decision you made?” Collect the two optional ratings independently, then ask why. Distinguish delight at a score from pride in their own reasoning.
6. Offer The reminder trial without further teaching. Record the chosen conclusion, connections, use of outside help and explanation of the different study design. This single task is a diagnostic observation, not a validated transfer test.
7. Ask them to choose what they would do next, including stopping. Do not require them to praise the product or promise a return.

## Questions the first study should resolve

| Hypothesis | Observe | If contradicted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The journey is understandable | Entry, clue selection and both decision panels completed without facilitator rescue | Simplify the failed step before adding content |
| Players interpret rather than match phrases | Player can explain a scored connection using the underlying record | Rewrite clues/distractors or use a small constructed response reviewed by a person |
| The investigation feels consequential | Player can name how their plan changed the comparison | Strengthen the visible consequence and reduce explanatory copy |
| Feedback creates ownership | Player identifies a specific contribution beyond the score | Put that action and its consequence before the points |
| Support remains acceptable | Players understand hint rules and seek help when stuck | Revisit the independent-credit policy if it suppresses useful practice |
| The follow-up tests a different inference | Player distinguishes observational overlap from random assignment | Revise the check; do not claim transfer from a repeated template |

Use failures in this small sample to guide revisions. Do not turn five interviews into population-level percentages or causal claims.

## Return and learning need separate studies

With consent, make the prototype available for a week. Observe voluntary return, the chosen next mission and the reason for returning. Keep prompted follow-ups distinguishable from spontaneous use. Record friction and boredom, not only completions. Local history can support a debrief; no remote analytics are installed.

To evaluate learning later, develop multiple matched, unseen tasks with independent expert review, delayed assessment and an appropriate comparison condition. Predefine scoring and exclusions. A refreshed browser, replay or reset does not make a previously seen task unfamiliar. Do not infer general cognitive improvement from game-score growth.

## Commercial discovery

Treat the campaign-interest button as a conversation starter only. Show a concrete proposed chapter sequence to individual players; explore facilitated club sessions with a separate organisational buyer. Ask about existing substitutes, spending, decision process and a specific next step. Do not combine those buyer populations into a single demand claim.

For each authored mission, record hours spent on writing, evidence design, illustration, expert review, player testing and revisions. Estimate campaign production and maintenance costs from observed work before selecting prices. Validate willingness to pay separately; there is no checkout or paid commitment in this prototype.

## Decision before expansion

Prioritise understanding and perceived ownership in one strong mission before building a larger world. Retain breadth as a clearly labelled roadmap. Real-time multiplayer, paid campaigns and automated free-text assessment require their own design, safety, reliability and economics work.
